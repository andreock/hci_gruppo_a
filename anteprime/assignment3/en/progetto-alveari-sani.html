About the project

The problem

In recent years many beekeepers in Piedmont have lost part of their colonies. Parasites, diseases and sudden changes in temperature weaken bee colonies, and the damage is often discovered only when the hive is opened and it is too late.

For people who make a living from honey it is a financial loss. For everyone else it is one more risk for the pollination of orchards, vegetable gardens and wild plants.

What the group is doing

The ApiLab group installs weight, temperature and sound sensors in the hives of volunteer beekeepers. A hive that suddenly loses weight, gets too warm or changes its hum can signal a problem before it can be seen with the naked eye.

Meanwhile, the lab tests samples of bees and honey to find out which sensor signals really point to a disease. The first two hives are already active and send data every hour.

What it will leave behind

Beekeepers will receive practical, free advice in time to act, along with guides written in simple language.

The data will be public: anyone will be able to see how the bees in their area are doing.
