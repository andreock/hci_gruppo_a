About the project

The problem

To read text on a computer, blind people use a screen reader, the program that reads aloud what is on the screen, or a braille display. But in a PDF a formula is often a silent image: the program skips it, or reads it as a row of meaningless symbols.

This makes following a science course much harder, and many students give up.

What the group did

The MathAccess group developed a free, open converter that turns the formulas in lecture notes into a form that a screen reader reads clearly. The formula can also be explored step by step: first the overall structure, then the individual parts.

The group includes an accessibility consultant who is blind. The software was tested with 30 blind and visually impaired students from five universities, and some examples were printed in braille.

The results

The converter was released in September 2025 with accessible documentation and was presented in three schools. Anyone can use it free of charge.
