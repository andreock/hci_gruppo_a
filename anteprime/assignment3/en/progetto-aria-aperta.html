About the project

The problem

Official monitoring stations are few and far from schools. Yet that is where children and teenagers spend much of their day, often close to busy roads.

As a result, many schools do not know how clean the air is outside their own entrance, especially at the times when students and families come and go.

What the group will do

The Aria Aperta group will work with ten classes to build twenty low-cost sensors for fine particles and nitrogen dioxide, using open software and designs. Students will assemble the sensors in workshops and install them near school entrances.

The data will go to a public map, updated every hour. The sensors do not replace official stations, but they add measurements where there are none today. The first one is already on: class 3B assembled it in front of their school.

What it will leave behind

The map will stay online for one year. All the material will be available in Italian and English, so that other schools and other cities can repeat the project.
