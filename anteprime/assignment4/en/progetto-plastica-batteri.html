About the project

The problem

Microplastics are everywhere, including the rivers of Piedmont. They are fragments so small that water treatment plants cannot hold them all back.

Some bacteria can use them as food, but they are still little known: we do not know which are the most effective, or whether they could be useful outside the lab.

What the group did

The PlastiBio group collected samples along the Po, isolated the most promising bacteria and read their DNA. It then tested them in small bioreactors, containers in which you can monitor how they grow and how much plastic they consume.

Donations above the goal made it possible to extend sampling to Lake Viverone. A student also took part in the work, with a summer grant.

The results

The group isolated three strains that reduce polyethylene microplastics by 12% in 30 days. The results are in a paper under review.

This is not a ready-made solution yet: the next step is to find out how to use these bacteria in water treatment plants.
